My experience

Program leadership

Here are examples of programs and projects I led. Like every good project, these were built with extraordinary partnerships with colleagues whose passion, dedication, innovation, and hard work helped us improve behavioral health care for older Veterans.

Select VA programs

Home Based Primary Care Mental Health Integration

At VA Central Office I supported the integration of psychologists and psychiatrists who were newly joining Home Based Primary Care teams nationally. I led a national community of practice with an active listserv, regular virtual meetings, policy guidance, a SharePoint for resource sharing, a program FAQ, and a peer mentorship program. With many partners we completed two national program surveys and multiple quality improvement projects, including a guide to measurement based mental health care, documentation guidance, and team-based treatment planning guidance.

Community Living Center Mental Health Integration

Community Living Centers are VA nursing home care settings, with rehabilitation, long-term, respite, and hospice care services. At VA Central Office I provided national support for the integration of psychologists and psychiatrists onto CLC teams, and led the community of practice that supported them. With many partners we developed program resources, including guidance for developing short-stay behavioral care services, a CLC psychologist guidebook, a monthly interdisciplinary continuing education webinar series, and a national program survey.

National program survey

STAR-VA

STAR-VA is a team-based behavioral intervention that helps Community Living Center teams understand and manage dementia-related distress behaviors among residents with dementia. It was adapted from Dr. Linda Teri’s Staff Training in Assisted Living Residences intervention. For over a decade I coordinated a national training program for CLC psychologists and registered nurses, who learned to engage the entire team in supporting residents with dementia. As is common with long-term projects, we encountered many challenges and opportunities along the way, not only in training teams but in sustaining the care approach and learning from evaluation over time.

Care for Patients with Complex Problems

In 2014 I was invited to co-chair a national VHA workgroup to address the needs of aging Veterans with complex medical, psychiatric, and neurological comorbidities whose behavioral concerns led to long inpatient stays and difficult discharge dispositions. Over time this work entailed conducting needs assessments, making recommendations about integrated behavioral care, and identifying promising practices for dissemination. This effort focused on meeting the needs of some of the most complex and vulnerable in our care.

One of the most promising practices we identified was the Behavioral Recovery Outreach team model, an innovation originating at VA Central Iowa. BRO teams support transitions of care for Veterans with dementia or serious mental illness whose behavioral concerns can interfere with discharge planning. I partnered closely with the program’s leader to explore national policy and standard operating procedures, potential legislative changes, staff education, and evaluation as the model spread regionally and then to other interested VA medical centers nationally.

Ask-the-Expert Geriatric Psychiatry

As part of VHA’s National Expert Consultation and Specialized Services program, we initiated virtual consultation to enhance access to specialized geriatric psychiatry services. I advocated for the program’s development and identified experts to provide one of two services: answering email questions from VA clinicians about psychiatric care of older Veterans, and providing video telehealth sessions with Veterans, caregivers, and teams around the country. I also advocated for expansion of regional geriatric telemental health services and advised a national evaluation team.

Regional Geriatric Mental Health Champions

One of the challenges of leading within a large national healthcare system is ensuring strong two-way communication between the field and national leaders, and being able to identify challenges and promising practices nationwide. For success, I needed regional partners to help with communication, strategy, and planning. In 2019 I invited the 18 Veterans Integrated Services Network regional leaders to nominate a Geriatric Mental Health Champion for their region. The champions went on to conduct regional needs assessments and set annual goals for supporting geriatric mental health communities of practice and programs.

Training, mentoring, and research

The Pikes Peak Model for Training in Professional Geropsychology

One highlight of my professional service was the opportunity to co-chair the 2006 National Conference on Training in Professional Geropsychology, which resulted in publication of the competency-based Pikes Peak Model. I then led a Council of Professional Geropsychology Training Programs workgroup to develop a tool for evaluating these competencies, the Pikes Peak Geropsychology Knowledge and Skill Assessment Tool. This model and its related tools have influenced geropsychology training nationwide at the graduate, internship, fellowship, and post-licensure levels.

Education at scale

For many years I and an interprofessional planning committee coordinated a monthly national continuing education geriatric mental health webinar series, open to all VHA staff. We sought feedback on learning needs, chose annual themes, identified expert speakers, and tracked evaluation feedback. I also initiated a project to develop a core knowledge curriculum in geriatric mental health, which a talented interprofessional team has since brought to reality.

Training and mentoring

Between 1996 and 2013 at VA Boston I supervised 38 geropsychology postdoctoral fellows and 28 psychology interns, many of whom are now leaders in this field. I directed clinical geropsychology training there from 1998 to 2011. In my role at VA Central Office, I mentored many colleagues taking on project or leadership responsibilities and, from 2016 to 2025, I offered a weekly consultation group for VA psychologists seeking board certification in geropsychology.

Scientific partnership

Over my entire career I enjoyed collaborating on research to enhance our knowledge of complex problems related to health and well-being in late life. My primary areas of research interest and collaboration have included the assessment and communication of values related to health care decision making, decision-making capacity in late life, competencies for professional geropsychology practice, mental health among older Veterans, and integrated care models. Further, I have written widely on topics including ethics, geropsychology training, workforce development, and mental health and aging.
